## New source since v0.5.77

### Visible racing vehicle details

- Procedural wheel rolling from real signed longitudinal Chaos chassis
  motion: forward and reverse roll in opposite directions, bounded
  against physics hitches, malformed velocities and teleport jumps.
- The front decorative wheels visually steer alongside the existing
  player control policy; rear wheels stay straight. Wheel axes are
  oriented along the vehicle's real axle rather than forward along
  the body.
- Lightweight collision-free cube spokes on each cylinder wheel make
  rotation visible without third-party wheel art. These are visual
  decorations, not simulated tires, wheel friction or suspension.
- The same car pawn source also supplies the prototype rival car;
  vehicle forces, collision, official lap timing and progression
  remain independent of visual animation.

### More responsive offline rival behavior

- A C++20 player-traffic policy reads the real player pawn's location
  and world velocity, compares it with the rival's measured heading
  and speed, and conservatively reduces AI throttle or brakes when
  approaching a slower car **directly in the lane**.
- The rule preserves the existing route planner, corner braking,
  waypoint arrival, pause/start/physics readiness and on-track race
  clock. It does not generate traffic cars or attempt risky swerves,
  automatic overtakes or official rival race placement.

### Tests and scope

- Strict three-OS C++20 regression tests for signed wheel roll,
  steering, resets, malformed physics and 100,000 short samples;
  player-facing rival traffic detection, relative closing speed,
  heading projection, lane limits and malformed states.
- Python source-contract tests confirm these policies are invoked by
  actual Unreal pawn and rival Tick code, not just detached examples.
- Documentation and explicit source-only visual/AI QA plans.
