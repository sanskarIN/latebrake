## Changes since v0.5.82

### RIVAL GRIP — physically sampled AI corner pace
- The actual autonomous rival now uses its last valid race-local arcade
  tire grip condition, measured forward/sideways chassis velocity and
  real route steering demand to lower throttle or request ordinary
  braking before a corner. Its path remains the existing four-gate
  circuit route; there is no invented overtaking path or rubber-banding.
- Grip risk is qualified only from valid short physics samples,
  meaningful steering and measured slip/grip loss. Straight sections,
  low-speed launch and already-authoritative checkpoint/corner braking
  retain existing priority and can still progress.
- A strictly bounded **0.12-second** AI corner brake hold helps avoid
  oscillation at the speed threshold. Race reset, interrupted or
  unready physics, route completion, checkpoint recovery and malformed
  telemetry clear transient state instead of permitting stale motor
  acceleration.
- The new policy cannot add motor force, change steering, rewrite
  opponent progress, modify credits, records or saves, or bypass the
  previously qualified player-aware rival following policy.
- This is an **arcade source model**, not a validated suspension/tire
  solver, real-world braking predictor or confirmed physical Chaos
  driving result. See
  [Rival grip pacing QA](https://github.com/sanskarIN/latebrake/blob/v0.5.83/docs/RIVAL_GRIP_PACE_QA.md).

### Existing source mechanics preserved
- **ARCADE TIRE** run-local heat/wear policy remains shared by both
  chassis, with a bounded grip reduction and uninterrupted competition.
- **NITRO BOOST** is still a human-only, finite-energy held speed
  advantage and never becomes an autonomous rival ability.
- **SLIPSTREAM** still requires genuine geometry behind the actual
  moving rival; its combined force with human nitro stays capped at 40%.
- Checkpoint timing, career progression, local player authority, source
  package provenance and release-asset policy are unchanged.
